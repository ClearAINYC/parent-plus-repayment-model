How to use this

1 · Enter & save a loan

On the first tab, fill in everything that affects the loan: the balances and rates, income and household, retirement plans, tax rates, whether the borrower qualifies for PSLF, and the assumptions (poverty figures). Hover any ? to see what a field does. Give it a name and press Save loan. It's stored in your browser; use Download backup to keep a copy or move it to another computer.

2 · Optimize

Open the Optimize tab, pick a saved loan, and choose what "best" means (lowest total cost, lowest payment now, lowest payment in retirement, most forgiven, fastest payoff). The tool builds every strategy, crowns the winner, and explains it.

3 · Compare two loans

Save two versions (say "consolidate now" vs "wait", or two different incomes), then the Compare tab puts them side by side with the dollar differences and both balance curves.

Nothing is assumed silently — if it changes the math, it's a field you control. The Google/Excel model and the written guide cover the same logic.

Parent PLUS repayment under current law — June 2026

The key fact: a Parent PLUS loan can only use Standard, Graduated, and Extended on its own. To reach any income-based plan (ICR, then IBR) or PSLF, you must consolidate into a Direct Consolidation Loan and enroll in ICR — and under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, that consolidation must be disbursed by June 30, 2026. After that, income-based enrollment closes for Parent PLUS for good.

Plans

Standard: level payment, 10 years (the only fixed plan that counts for PSLF). Extended: up to 25 years, needs >$30k. Consolidation-standard: 12–30 years by balance. ICR: 20% of income above 100% of poverty, forgiven at 25 years — the only income-based plan a consolidated Parent PLUS can enter. IBR: Old = 15% above 150% of poverty (25-yr); New = 10% (20-yr); capped at the 10-year standard amount. RAP (new, 7/1/2026): Direct loans only — Parent PLUS not eligible.

Forgiveness & taxes

PSLF: 120 qualifying payments with a government/nonprofit employer → tax-free. Income-based forgiveness (20–30 yrs) is federally taxable again as of Jan 1, 2026. This tool estimates the tax as forgiven balance × your combined marginal rate.

Key dates

Jan 1, 2026 income-based forgiveness taxable. Apr 1, 2026 recommended consolidation application. Jun 30, 2026 consolidation must be disbursed for income-based/PSLF access. Jul 1, 2026 RAP opens; tiered Standard; borrowing caps. Jul 1, 2028 ICR/PAYE/SAVE end.

Reflects statute and Department of Education guidance as understood June 2026; regulations are still being written. Confirm with your servicer and a tax professional.

Assumptions & simplifications

  • ICR uses the 20%-of-discretionary-income branch. Standard/Extended/Consolidation and income-based formulas are exact; interest compounds annually here (matches the sheet).
  • "Income-based only after retirement" counts the forgiveness clock from when repayment began (borrower-favorable). Confirm timing with your servicer.
  • IBR for Parent PLUS defaults to Old IBR (15%/25-yr). Taxes are a flat estimate; PSLF is tax-free.
  • Projections under today's rules; law, rates, and poverty figures change yearly.
