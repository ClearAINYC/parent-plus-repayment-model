How to use this model

This is a template. Every number on the Model tab is a placeholder. Replace the placeholders with real figures, click Recalculate, and read the comparison table. Nothing you type is stored or transmitted — it lives only in this browser tab.

Step 1 — Enter your loans

Add one row per Parent PLUS loan, or a single blended row. If you type the origination year, the model fills that year's official Direct PLUS interest rate; overwrite it if your statement shows a different rate (older FFEL or variable-rate loans differ). The blended balance and weighted-average rate appear below the rows.

Step 2 — Set household, income, and the retirement step-down

Family size drives the poverty-line deduction used by income-driven plans. AGI and AGI growth project your income forward. If you expect income to drop at retirement, fill in years until retirement and retirement AGI — income-driven payments will recalculate from that point (they can fall to $0 if retirement income is low enough).

Step 3 — Choose strategy options

Toggle PSLF if the parent borrower works full-time for government or a 501(c)(3). Keep Consolidation on to see the income-driven pathways — Parent PLUS loans cannot reach any IDR plan without first consolidating. The poverty figures are editable for Alaska/Hawaii or future-year updates.

Step 4 — Read the comparison

Each row is a full repayment pathway. Total cost = everything you pay plus estimated tax on any forgiven balance. The green-shaded row is the lowest total cost given your inputs. The "Eligible" column flags what's actually available to you (Parent PLUS is more restricted than student-held loans).

Step 5 — Compare scenarios & inspect the schedule

Click Save these inputs as a scenario to snapshot the current assumptions, change something (income, consolidation timing, retirement age), save again, and open the Saved Scenarios tab to see them side by side. The Amortization tab shows the year-by-year schedule for any single pathway.

What decision is this built to support?

Four questions, in order: (1) Should you consolidate before June 30, 2026 to keep income-driven repayment and PSLF on the table? (2) Which pathway gives the lowest lifetime cost? (3) Which gives the lowest monthly payment if cash flow is the constraint? (4) Does forgiveness (PSLF at 120 payments, or IDR forgiveness at 20–30 years) beat paying the loan off — after accounting for the tax bomb?

Parent PLUS repayment under current law — June 2026

The single most important fact: A Parent PLUS loan, by itself, can only use the Standard, Graduated, and Extended plans. To reach any income-driven plan or PSLF, you must consolidate into a Direct Consolidation Loan and enroll in ICR. Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21), that door closes for new enrollments on July 1, 2026 — the consolidation must be disbursed by June 30, 2026.

The two worlds: before vs. on/after July 1, 2026

Existing Parent PLUS loans (first borrowed before 7/1/2026): keep access to Standard / Graduated / Extended, and — only through a one-time consolidation done in time — ICR, which is being converted to Income-Based Repayment (IBR). IBR for most parent borrowers is "Old IBR": 15% of discretionary income, forgiveness at 25 years.

New Parent PLUS loans (first disbursed on/after 7/1/2026): only the new tiered Standard plan. No IDR, no PSLF. New loans are also capped at $20,000/year and $65,000 lifetime per student. Taking any new Parent PLUS loan for 2026–27 disqualifies you from IDR even on previously consolidated loans.

The repayment plans

  • Standard (current): level payment, 10 years. The only fixed plan that counts for PSLF.
  • Standard (new tiered, from 7/1/2026): 10 yrs (<$25k), 15 yrs ($25–50k), 20 yrs ($50–100k), 25 yrs ($100k+). Terms over 10 years do not count for PSLF.
  • Graduated / Extended: lower or stretched payments (Extended needs >$30k); approximations here. Eliminated for borrowers entering repayment after 7/1/2026.
  • ICR (Income-Contingent): 20% of income above 100% of poverty; 25-yr forgiveness. The only IDR a consolidated Parent PLUS loan can enter. Being phased out by 7/1/2028 (converting to IBR).
  • IBR: Old IBR = 15% of income above 150% of poverty, 25-yr forgiveness; New IBR = 10%, 20-yr. Payment capped at the 10-yr Standard amount. OBBBA removed the "partial financial hardship" gate.
  • RAP (Repayment Assistance Plan, from 7/1/2026): 1%–10% of AGI by bracket, −$50/dependent, $10 minimum, no negative amortization, 30-yr forgiveness. Direct loans only — Parent PLUS and consolidations that repaid Parent PLUS are NOT eligible. Shown here for reference only.

Forgiveness & taxes

PSLF: 120 qualifying payments on an eligible plan while working for a qualifying employer → tax-free forgiveness. For Parent PLUS this means consolidate → ICR/IBR → 120 payments, and it must be set up before the 2026 deadline.

IDR forgiveness (20–30 yrs) is again federally taxable as of January 1, 2026 — the American Rescue Plan's tax exclusion expired 12/31/2025. A large forgiven balance can trigger a sizable "tax bomb." This model estimates it as forgiven balance × your marginal rate.

Key dates

Aug 1, 2025 interest resumed for SAVE-forbearance borrowers. Jan 1, 2026 IDR forgiveness taxable again. Apr 1, 2026 recommended consolidation application date. Jun 30, 2026 consolidation must be disbursed for IDR/PSLF access. Jul 1, 2026 RAP opens; new tiered Standard begins; Parent PLUS borrowing caps apply. Jul 1, 2028 ICR/PAYE/SAVE fully eliminated.

This summary reflects statute and Department of Education guidance as understood in June 2026. Implementation dates have shifted before and regulations are still being written. Assumptions & simplifications

  • ICR uses the "20% of discretionary income" branch. The actual ICR payment is the lesser of that or a 12-year fixed amount × an income factor, so real ICR can be slightly lower at low incomes.
  • Graduated & Extended payments are modeled approximations (servicers compute the exact step schedule). Standard, Consolidation-Standard, and all IDR formulas are exact.
  • Interest capitalization is simplified: ICR capitalizes unpaid interest annually; IBR is treated as accruing-but-uncapitalized. Real-world capitalization events (plan exit, losing eligibility) can change forgiven balances.
  • IBR for Parent PLUS defaults to Old IBR (15%/25-yr). New IBR (10%/20-yr) is shown but generally requires "new borrower" status that most parent borrowers don't have.
  • Taxes are a flat marginal-rate estimate on forgiven balances; PSLF is tax-free. Consult a tax professional.
  • Figures are projections under today's rules; law, rates, and poverty guidelines change annually.
