How to use this

Switch between Simple and Advanced at the top right. Simple shows the essentials; Advanced exposes every lever (income growth, family size, taxes, poverty figures, payment history, and a custom-strategy builder). Hover any ? to see what a field does and which way it pushes your payment.

1. Enter the loans and your situation

Add the Parent PLUS loans (type, drag/drop, or paste). Put in the household income (AGI), and, if you want to see the retirement effect, how many years until retirement and the income you expect then. Press Update results.

2. Pick what "best" means

Use the "Find the strategy with the…" dropdown to rank by lowest total cost, lowest payment now, lowest payment in retirement, most forgiven, or fastest payoff. The winner gets a ★ and a plain-English recommendation appears.

3. Compare head-to-head

Pick any two strategies (for example Consolidate → income-based vs Don't consolidate → standard) to see them side by side with the differences, and watch both balances on the chart.

4. Try the real questions

Consolidate or not? Income-based now, or only after retirement? Go for PSLF? Build any of these in the custom-strategy box (Advanced) and it joins the comparison. Change one input at a time to see how a life choice moves the outcome.

The companion Google Sheet and the written guide cover the same logic. This page keeps everything in your browser — nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Parent PLUS repayment under current law — June 2026

The key fact: a Parent PLUS loan can only use Standard, Graduated, and Extended on its own. To reach any income-based plan (ICR, then IBR) or PSLF, you must consolidate into a Direct Consolidation Loan and enroll in ICR — and under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, that consolidation must be disbursed by June 30, 2026. After that, income-based enrollment closes for Parent PLUS for good.

Plans

Standard: level payment, 10 years (the only fixed plan that counts for PSLF). Extended: up to 25 years, needs >$30k. Consolidation-standard: 12–30 years by balance. ICR: 20% of income above 100% of poverty, forgiven at 25 years — the only income-based plan a consolidated Parent PLUS can enter. IBR: Old = 15% above 150% of poverty (25-yr); New = 10% (20-yr); payment capped at the 10-year standard amount. RAP (new, from 7/1/2026): Direct loans only — Parent PLUS is not eligible, shown for reference.

Forgiveness & taxes

PSLF: 120 qualifying payments with a government/nonprofit employer → tax-free. Income-based forgiveness (20–30 yrs) is federally taxable again as of Jan 1, 2026, so a big forgiven balance can mean a sizable "tax bomb." This tool estimates it as forgiven balance × your marginal rate.

Key dates

Jan 1, 2026 income-based forgiveness taxable again. Apr 1, 2026 recommended consolidation application date. Jun 30, 2026 consolidation must be disbursed for income-based/PSLF access. Jul 1, 2026 RAP opens; new tiered Standard; Parent PLUS borrowing caps. Jul 1, 2028 ICR/PAYE/SAVE end.

Reflects statute and Department of Education guidance as understood in June 2026; regulations are still being written and dates have shifted before. Confirm with your servicer at studentaid.gov and a tax professional.

Assumptions & simplifications

  • ICR uses the 20%-of-discretionary-income branch (real ICR is the lesser of that or a 12-yr factor, so it can be a bit lower at low incomes).
  • Graduated is omitted here for clarity; Standard, Extended, Consolidation-standard, and all income-based formulas are exact.
  • Interest compounds annually in this view; the spreadsheet matches. Capitalization is simplified (ICR capitalizes yearly; IBR accrues uncapitalized).
  • "Income-based only after retirement" counts the forgiveness clock from when repayment began (time-in-repayment), the borrower-favorable reading. Confirm timing with your servicer.
  • IBR for Parent PLUS defaults to Old IBR (15%/25-yr). Taxes are a flat estimate; PSLF is tax-free.
  • Projections under today's rules; law, rates, and poverty figures change yearly.
